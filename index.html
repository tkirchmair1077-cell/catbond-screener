4 · Methodology

How to read this page

Circles are earthquakes of magnitude 5.8 or greater in the snapshot window. Size is magnitude; colour is the USGS PAGER overall alert level (green, yellow, orange, red). Grey means PAGER has not been published. Translucent overlays, when present, are publicly sourced catastrophe‑bond coverage regions. This snapshot has no verified coverage zones, so every event is marked “no overlap”.

What the colours are not. PAGER is a USGS estimate of humanitarian and economic impact. It is not an insured‑loss figure, not an industry‑loss index, and not a contract result.

USGS PAGER modelled economic loss. Each event may carry a USD figure from the public PAGER product, shown on the map tooltip, in the event panel and in the table, using the snapshot’s own display text. The label on this page is: “USGS PAGER modelled economic loss: all losses, not insured loss, not a bond trigger.” The 50% and 90% ranges attached to that figure are this desk’s own calculation (d), not a USGS published range. Missing values are shown as n/a. These figures are never used as a contractual measure or a bond trigger.

The loss ladder stays separate. Economic loss, insured (industry) loss and bond principal impairment are three different quantities and are kept apart. This public page does not publish insured‑loss figures or numeric principal‑impairment estimates. A principal‑loss figure would appear only when the contractual measure and the attachment and exhaustion points have all been verified from deal documents — and those terms stay off this page.

Phase 1 scope. Detection uses the USGS earthquake catalogue (ComCat via the FDSN API). Alert‑band events are M>6.0 (strictly greater); watch band events are 5.8≤M≤6.0. Public status values are “no overlap”, “overlap, assessment pending”, or “threshold met”. The last of these is used only with a stated public basis; Phase 1 has no such criterion. The map and table show the status carried in the snapshot; they do not recompute it.

Tropical-cyclone demo. When storms.json is present, the map can show official National Hurricane Center (NHC) forecast cones and tracks, the NHC observed best track, and Google DeepMind Weather Lab ensemble member tracks for a named demo storm. Official NHC products are U.S. Government works (public domain). Weather Lab tracks are experimental research data, not official, licensed CC BY 4.0. The cone is not the wind, surge or rain footprint. A track entering a coarse country outline is a watch condition, not a payout. The relevance state “REPORTED TRIGGER OUTCOME” is used only when a public source reports that outcome, and is cited. Bond fields on the Jamaica zone are a public summary, not verified against an offering circular, and are flagged as a demo/example. Official NHC and Weather Lab results are shown side by side and are never blended.

Data and sources: caveats

    Data: USGS Earthquake Hazards Program / ComCat and PAGER. Event catalogue via the FDSN event API.

    Tropical-cyclone demo: official forecast cones, forecast tracks and best track from the NOAA National Hurricane Center (U.S. Government work, public domain). Ensemble member tracks: Google DeepMind Weather Lab (WeatherNext Cyclones), CC BY 4.0, experimental research data, not official. Cone is not the wind, surge or rain footprint.

    This page is a public research view of a Phase 1 geographic screen. It is not investment advice, and it does not publish insured‑loss figures, deal terms or principal‑impairment estimates. Magnitudes, locations, depths and PAGER levels can be revised by USGS.
